Document blueprint

Reading the Surface

A plan for the explanatory document that tells curators, conservators and cataloguers what HELIO Pro does to a manuscript page — and how it does it.

Subject HELIO Pro 2.6.0 Target length 3,000–4,000 words Sections 12 + 3 appendices Status outline for approval

The brief, before the structure

The document has one job: convince a reader who has never met photometric stereo that HELIO produces evidence about a surface, not enhanced photographs — and show them what that evidence looks like on a real manuscript.

Three audiences read it differently. A curator wants to know what it finds. A conservator wants to know what it measures and whether it is repeatable. A digitisation lead wants to know what it costs in time, kit and storage, and what standards it outputs. Write for the curator; put the conservator's answers in §7 and the digitisation lead's in §10 and the appendices, so each can skip to their own.

The single most important sentence in the whole document is the distinction in §2. Everything else is elaboration.

§ 01
ArguesRaking light already works — but it is unrepeatable and needs the object.
FigureSame opening under two hand-held torch angles.

What is hard to see, and how it is seen now

Open on something the reader already does. Every codicologist has tilted a page toward a window or run a torch across it at a low angle, because relief that is invisible under flat light appears instantly under raking light: ruling, pricking, scoring, the ghost of an erased line, the impression of writing from the facing leaf.

Then name the three limitations, without disparaging the practice:

  • It is not repeatable. The angle lives in the hand of whoever held the torch. Two people describing the same feature cannot be sure they lit it the same way.
  • It is not shareable. The observation happens in the reading room. A photograph fixes one angle and throws the rest away.
  • It requires the object. Every re-examination is another consultation of a fragile item.
The turnThese are not problems with raking light. They are problems with raking light being a gesture rather than a record.
§ 02
ArguesHELIO records the surface itself, so the light becomes adjustable afterwards.
LengthTwo paragraphs. Do not elaborate here.

What HELIO Pro actually produces

The load-bearing distinction, stated once and plainly: HELIO does not enhance a photograph. It photographs the page several times from a fixed camera with the light moved between shots, and from the differences in brightness it computes which way every point on the surface faces. From that it reconstructs the relief.

The output is a measurement of the surface. The light is no longer baked in — it becomes a parameter the reader can move, in a browser, years later, with the manuscript back in its box.

Sentence to carry the documentRaking light stops being something you do to a manuscript and becomes something you store about it.
§ 03
ArguesThe physics is simple enough to state honestly in one page.
FigureThree frames lit from three sides, plus the resulting normal map.

How it works, without the mathematics

A matte surface lit by a lamp is brightest where it faces the lamp squarely and darkens as it turns away. That relationship is predictable. Photograph the same page under three or more known light directions and each pixel gives you several brightness readings, which together pin down the direction that pixel faces.

Do that for every pixel and you have a normal map — a record of surface orientation. Integrate the orientations across the page and you have a height map: the relief itself, in relative units until it is calibrated.

Keep the single equation out of the body text. Put it in Appendix C for readers who want it. Two constraints do need stating in the body, because they govern capture:

  • The camera must not move between frames. Only the light moves.
  • The light directions must be known — which is the whole of §4.
§ 04
ArguesAccuracy is decided here, not in the software.
FigureThe 1A / 1B / 1C panel; a chrome sphere with its highlight ringed.

Where the lights were

The reconstruction is only as good as the light directions fed into it. HELIO offers three routes, and the document should be candid that they are not equivalent.

RouteWhenAccuracy
1A External lightsA dome rig with a known geometry, or an existing .lp fileAs good as the rig
1B Sphere calibrationA reflective sphere is in shotBest. Directions are measured from your own frames
1C Virtual domeNeither of the above — a legacy or field setApproximate; positions are estimated by hand

Explain why 1B wins: the highlight on a mirrored sphere is a direct read-out of the lamp's direction, recorded in the same frames as the object. Nothing has to be remembered, measured on site, or trusted from a previous session. Recommend that a sphere be included in every capture as standard practice, exactly as a colour target is.

Do not overclaimHELIO's automatic sphere detection is explicitly experimental and the interface says so. The document should say so too, and describe the confirm-and-adjust step rather than implying it is hands-off.
§ 05
ArguesThe findings section. This is what most readers came for.
RuleOne claim per figure. No claim without one.

What it finds on a manuscript

The heart of the document, and the part most likely to overreach. Organise by what the feature is, not by which button produced it, and attach a figure to every claim.

Preparation of the page — ruling and pricking, whether blind-ruled or in lead; the pattern of prickings at the edge; bounding lines; the impression of a stylus that left no pigment at all.

Writing and its removal — the furrow a nib leaves in the surface; scraping and erasure, which flattens or thins the substrate; corrections written over a prepared area. Note carefully that HELIO shows the topography of an erasure, not the chemistry of what was removed.

Structure and handling — sewing stations, cockling, offsets and set-off from a facing leaf, losses and tears, insect grazing, the shadow of a former binding.

Impressions and applied material — seal impressions and their tooling, binding stamps and rolls, gilding and its ground.

Boundary to state explicitlyThese are the categories the method is suited to. Which of them a given capture actually resolves depends on the relief being deep enough to measure — a point §9 has to make honestly rather than leave to the reader's disappointment.
§ 06
ArguesEach view answers a different question; the reader can choose deliberately.
FigureOne opening, four views, same crop.

Choosing what to look at

HELIO holds many views of the same solved surface. A reader who does not know why will treat the list as a menu of filters. Frame it as a set of questions instead:

QuestionView
What does it look like, lit from here?Colour relighting, driven by the light dome
Which way does the surface face?Normal map
How deep is that groove?Depth, with the profile tool
Where is the relief, ignoring the ink?The relief engines
What does the whole capture say at once?Stack-derived views

Two practical points that repay their space: use a low light — most disappointment with the tool is someone lighting from overhead and concluding there is nothing there; and the stack-derived views deliberately ignore the light slider, because they summarise the whole capture rather than depict one lighting.

§ 07
ArguesIt measures. This is the section conservators read first.
FigureA depth profile across a ruled line, in millimetres.

Measurement, not just illustration

The distinction that separates HELIO from image enhancement, and the strongest argument for conservation use. Once a scale is set from a target in frame, the surface carries real units.

  • Depth profiles across a groove, a score or a loss.
  • Volume and true 3D surface area over a defined region.
  • Change detection against a previous session's depth — the basis for monitoring a condition over years rather than describing it in prose.
  • Solver quality reporting, so a reader can tell where the reconstruction is trustworthy and where it is not.
Frame it this wayA condition report that says "slight cockling, stable" is a judgement. One that carries a depth profile and a date is a measurement someone can repeat in ten years.

Emphasise the first row. A curator who can send a relightable page to a colleague, and have it open with no installation, is the single most persuasive demonstration the software has.

§ 09
ArguesCredibility. State the limits before a reader discovers them.
TonePlain and unapologetic. No hedging.

What it does not do

The section that earns the rest its trust. Name each limit and, where possible, name the technique that does address it.

  • It is not multispectral imaging. It records topography, not ink chemistry. An erasure shows as a change in the surface; recovering what the text said is a different instrument.
  • Depth is relative until calibrated. Without a scale target in frame, comparisons within one capture hold but absolute figures do not.
  • Shiny and translucent materials break the assumption. Gilding, glaze and heavily burnished surfaces do not scatter light evenly, and the reconstruction is correspondingly less reliable there. HELIO can show where this is happening rather than hide it.
  • Very shallow relief may simply not resolve. Honest expectation-setting: the method finds what is physically there to find.
  • It is not a substitute for examining the object. It is a record that reduces how often the object must be brought out.
On the AI toolsHELIO includes AI-assisted description and detection features. In a document read by cataloguers, describe these as drafting aids requiring verification. Claiming them as findings would undermine §5, which is the section that has to be believed.
§ 10
ArguesIt is affordable and fits an existing studio.
NeedsReal timings from a session. Do not estimate.

What it takes in practice

Kit: a camera on a copy stand, one movable light, a reflective sphere, a scale target. No dome required — say so early, because the assumption that this needs a rig is the main barrier to anyone trying it.

Then the numbers a digitisation lead actually needs, measured rather than guessed: frames per opening, minutes to capture, minutes to process, gigabytes stored per opening at working and archival resolution.

§ 11
ArguesProof. One manuscript, beginning to end.
FigureThe whole sequence, captioned.

A worked example

One opening, followed from capture to conclusion: the frames as shot, the sphere calibration, the solve, the view that revealed the feature, the measurement taken, and the finished figure. Caption every step with what was done and why.

Choose an example where the finding is modest and real rather than spectacular. A ruling pattern recovered from a page where it was invisible is more convincing to a sceptical reader than a dramatic result they suspect was cherry-picked.

§ 12
ArguesNothing. It removes obstacles to reading the rest.

Glossary and further reading

Short definitions of the terms the document cannot avoid — photometric stereo, RTI, normal map, albedo, raking light, specular, Lambertian. Then references to the RTI literature and to cultural-heritage imaging guidance, so the document sits in a tradition rather than appearing self-contained.

The in-app help already carries a glossary in this register; reuse that wording so the document and the software speak the same language.

PurposeDetail that would break the argument if inline.

Appendices

  • A — Capture protocol. A one-page checklist that can be laminated and kept on the copy stand.
  • B — Output inventory. Every file HELIO writes, what it is for, and which are archival.
  • C — The method in full. The equation, the solver options, and what each assumes — for readers who want to assess rather than accept it.

Two things to settle before drafting. First, the worked example in §11 — the document is only as persuasive as that one sequence, and everything else should be written around it. Second, whether §5's claims can each be backed by a figure from your own captures; any that cannot should be cut rather than softened.
